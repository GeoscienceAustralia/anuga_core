
\section{A settling basin with flow}

Sediment-laden water enters a straight channel 300~m long and 10~m wide
carrying a uniform steady flow: a flat frictionless bed, depth $h = 1$~m
and velocity $u = 0.5$~m/s, fixed at both ends by Dirichlet boundaries that
hold the same state, so the discharge per unit width $q = uh = 0.5$~m$^2$/s
is exact. The inflow carries 100~$\mu$m silt at the volumetric concentration
$c_0 = 2\times10^{-4}$, with the settling velocity $v_s$ of Ferguson and
Church~\cite{FergusonChurch2004} ($v_s = 8.0$~mm/s); entrainment is switched
off by a zero critical Shields stress, and deposition is the well-mixed rate
$D = d^{*} v_s c$, $d^{*} = 1$. At steady state the depth-averaged
concentration obeys $d(qc)/dx = -d^{*} v_s c$, so
\begin{equation}
c(x) = c_0\, e^{-x/L_s}, \qquad L_s = \frac{q}{d^{*} v_s} = 62.5~\mathrm{m} ,
\end{equation}
and the bed rises where the sediment lands at the rate
$dz/dt = d^{*} v_s c(x)/(1-\lambda)$, $\lambda = 0.3$. As the bed rises the
depth falls and the velocity rises to keep $q$, so $L_s$ is unchanged and the
reference holds for the whole run; the bed rise stays a few millimetres.

The case checks the tracer advection scheme with an inflow boundary
concentration, the deposition term in the sediment mass balance, the bed
update under deposition, and that sediment is conserved between the
boundaries, the water column and the bed: the net inflow across the
boundaries must equal what is in suspension plus $(1-\lambda)$ times the
deposited volume. The steady profile is compared cell by cell at the end of
the run, the bed-rise rate over its last 100~s, and the budget to
$10^{-3}$; the tracer advection is first-order upwind, so the profile carries
an error of order $\Delta x / L_s$.
