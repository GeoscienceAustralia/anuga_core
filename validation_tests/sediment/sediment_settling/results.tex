
\section{Settling of suspended sediment in still water}

A tank of still water of depth $h_0 = 1$~m carries a uniform volumetric
concentration $c_0 = 0.01$ of a single sediment fraction (100~$\mu$m grains).
There is no flow, so the bed shear stress is zero and nothing is entrained;
the only exchange with the bed is deposition with a constant near-bed profile
factor $d^{*} = 1$,
\begin{equation}
D = d^{*} v_s c ,
\end{equation}
with the settling velocity $v_s$ from Ferguson and Church~\cite{FergusonChurch2004}.
The conserved variable is the sediment mass per unit area $m = hc$, and the
bed rises by the deposited volume divided by the packing fraction
$(1-\lambda)$, $\lambda = 0.3$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dm}{dt} = -D, \qquad \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{D}{1-\lambda} .
\end{equation}
The free surface does not move, so the depth $h = w - z$ falls as the bed
rises. With that feedback the system has no closed form and is integrated to
machine precision for the reference; without it the concentration decays
exponentially with e-folding time $h_0/(d^{*}v_s)$, which the reference
approaches as $c_0 \to 0$. The case checks the deposition term, its coupling
into the conserved sediment mass, and the bed update, and that sediment mass
is conserved between the water column and the bed.
